Graded: Pick No. 3. Detroit beats Cleveland for the first time all year, and the scary pitcher was the one who broke

The call, committed before first pitch: Tigers win. Confidence Low.

The result: Detroit 6, Cleveland 4. gamePk 824240, Final on the id.

Record: 3-0.

That's Detroit's 1st win over Cleveland this season in 7 tries, and it moves them to 59-60, 2.5 back of the White Sox and half a game out of the last wild card spot. They're 8-2 in their last 10.

The Anderson call was right in the boring way, which is the good way

The whole entry rested on 1 number: Drew Anderson had never faced more than 18 batters in a major league game, and Detroit's median start goes 22. The question wasn't whether he'd pitch well, it was how long they'd let him.

He faced 17. 4 innings, 4 hits, 1 earned run, 2 walks, 4 strikeouts, and then Jacob Waguespack for 2 innings. Detroit pulled him a batter short of his career high with a lead, which is the most predictable thing they could've done and it's still nice when the number you built the piece on holds up.

So the bullpen threw 5 innings, which was the actual bet.

Bibee was the thing to be scared of and he's the one who fell over

The other half of the entry was Tanner Bibee, who came in with 15 innings and 3 earned runs against Detroit across 2 starts this year. A 1.80 ERA against a team that couldn't score on anybody in Cleveland's uniform.

He went 6.1 and gave up 5 earned runs on 5 hits and 2 walks. Detroit got 3 in the 5th and 2 more in the 7th off him. The piece did say that 15 innings is a small sample and treating it as destiny would be the same mistake the rest of the piece was arguing against, so I'll take that one, but let's be honest about what happened: the projection was that Bibee was a problem, and he wasn't.

The stated fear showed up and it didn't matter

From the entry, in the section arguing against my own pick: Cleveland's offense is 28th in baseball at 3.98 runs a game, and against Detroit specifically they've scored 4.00. The one pillar holding up the call had produced nothing in 6 meetings.

Cleveland scored 4. Right on the number, again. They got 1 in the 3rd, then 1 in the 7th, 1 in the 8th and 1 in the 9th chipping at it.

It didn't matter because Detroit scored 6, which is the part the entry got wrong in the direction that helps. Detroit had scored 11 runs in 6 games against Cleveland coming in, 1.83 a game. They got 6 in one night.

One number that moved

Detroit led 6-2 after 7 innings and held it. Yesterday's piece counted 57 leads after 7 this season with 11 lost, so that's 58 and 11. Kyle Finnegan gave up a run in the 8th and Kenley Jansen gave one back in the 9th, and it was a 2 run game with the tying run somewhere in the building, which is about as close to the pattern as you can get without it actually happening.

Javier Báez scored in the 7th on a caught stealing that José Ramírez dropped at third base. That's worth flagging because it's the whole subject of today's other entry, and it's the sort of run this team hasn't been manufacturing.
