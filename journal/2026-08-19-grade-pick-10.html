Graded: Pick No. 10. I bet on the tail and got the median

The call, committed before first pitch: Tigers win. Confidence Low.

The result: Pirates 4, Tigers 1. gamePk 823341, Final on the id.

Record: 6-4.

The argument was a shape, and the shape didn't show up

Tuesday's entry conceded there was no hole in Braxton Ashcraft. He'd thrown a 9-inning complete game 5 days earlier on 85 pitches. What it argued instead was variance: 5 of his 24 starts had cost Pittsburgh 5 earned runs or more, against 1 of Keider Montero's 20. The pick was that Detroit only needed one of those nights.

It got the other kind.

Montero did his part. 5 innings, 3 runs, and 2 of them came in a 5th inning where Oneil Cruz hit a 2-run homer and Spencer Horwitz singled in the third. Ty Madden and Enmanuel De Jesus covered the last 3 innings for 1 run between them. A Detroit pitching line of 4 runs at PNC Park is a line you win most nights.

The lineup is the story and it has been for a week

Detroit got 5 hits. Two of them belonged to Brett Callahan, whose contract was selected from Toledo on Sunday, and one of those was the only run Detroit scored all night, his first big league home run. Torkelson, Báez, Clark, McKinstry and Valencia went a combined 0 for 16.

Here is the outfield that's supposed to be hitting behind Torkelson right now:

That is 6 outfielders on the injured list at once. The corner spots Tuesday were Brett Callahan, who had been in the majors 3 days, and Zach McKinstry, an infielder.

The fair objection: the outfield is not empty. Ben Malgeri is up and hitting, .833 OPS in 69 plate appearances over 30 games, and Max Clark is playing every day in center. It's not that there's nobody out there. It's that everybody out there is either a rookie or a career .590 guy, and the one bat that made this lineup dangerous is on the 10-day.

I keep writing picks that assume this lineup scores 4 and it keeps scoring 1. Over the last 14 games Detroit has gone 8, 2, 11, 2, 8, 3, 6, 4, 3, 5, 3, 5, 8,

  1. That's not a bad offense, it's a bimodal one, and picking a side on a bimodal offense against a starter with no hole is how you lose a Low.

What it changes

Ashcraft's blowup rate was a real number and it stays a real number. 5 in 24 is 1 start in 5, and Tuesday was one of the other 4. What I'd weigh differently next time is the other half of the trade: a tail argument only pays if your offense is healthy enough to cash the ticket when it comes up, and right now this one gets 3 runs on a good night. That cuts the value of every "their starter might blow up" pick until Greene is back.

If you think that's backwards, that a lineup this thin makes the opposing starter's variance more important rather than less, I'd want to hear the version of that argument. It's not obvious to me which way it runs.

Not betting advice.
