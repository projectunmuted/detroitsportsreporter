Four Numbers, Monday Aug 31: 27, 134-155, 60 and 41

This runs Monday mornings. One number for each of the Tigers, Lions, Pistons and Red Wings, whichever one is worth knowing that week, with the arithmetic shown so you can check it.

Tigers: 27

27 saves. 28 blown. 55 chances, and they've converted fewer than half of them.

Three clubs in baseball have blown more saves than they've converted this season, and Detroit is one of them. Here are the 5 worst conversion rates in the league; the top 3 rows are those 3 clubs.

The part that makes it strange is the company Detroit isn't keeping. The Angels have a bad pitching staff. Washington has a bad pitching staff. Detroit has the 5th best team ERA in baseball at 3.58, behind the Yankees, Milwaukee, Atlanta and Boston and ahead of 25 other clubs.

That's the whole season in 2 rows. 596 runs scored, 531 allowed, a plus 65 that should be worth something, and a 63-73 record that isn't. Sunday was a version of it in miniature: Framber Valdez left down 3-1 and Tanner Rainey turned it into 6-1 in a third of an inning.

Good pitching. Handed back after the 7th.

Lions: 134-155

That's the 2025 record of the 17 teams on Detroit's 2026 schedule, added up. A .464 winning percentage. Only 3 teams in the league drew a softer one: Cleveland at .429, New Orleans at .433 and Cincinnati at .450. Detroit is level with Atlanta and Indianapolis at .464, so call it 4th easiest of 32 in a 3 way tie.

5 of the 17 are against clubs that won 6 games or fewer last year. Three of them went 3-14. On the other side there are only 4 games against teams that won 11 or more, and 2 of those are the Chicago games.

Now the honest part, because last year's record is a weak instrument. It's the same lesson the preseason numbers teach: teams turn over, and what a club did in January tells you less about September than the confidence with which people quote it. Six of these games are against Green Bay, Minnesota and Chicago, and nobody needs a 2025 record to know those are hard.

What the number does say is that the soft part of this schedule is real and it is front loaded. The Jets, Carolina and Arizona in weeks 3, 4 and 5, with the bye in week 6. If Detroit is not comfortably over .500 by Halloween, the schedule will not have been the reason.

Pistons: 60

60-22. The most games the Pistons have won in a season since 2005-06, when they won 64.

Two years before that, they won 14.

Red Wings: 41

41 wins in 82 games. They also won 41 in 2024, and 41 in 2016.

Since 2015 the Red Wings have never won more than 41 games in a season. Three times they've landed on exactly 41, which is the kind of consistency nobody wants.

Two rebuilds, one city, the same 12 years. One of them bottomed out at 14 wins and came out the other side. The other one has spent a decade arriving at the same answer.

The hockey caveat matters here: an overtime loss is a loss in that win column but it's a point in the standings, so 41-41 understates a Red Wings season a little. It understates all 3 of them by about the same amount, which is why they still line up.

Both seasons start in October. This is the last quiet Monday either of them gets.
